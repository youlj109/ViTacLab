Overview

From contact physics to learned manipulation

ViTacLab studies how robotic control shifts from vision toward touch across parallel grippers, dexterous hands, and bimanual systems.

Abstract

One stack, three connected research problems.

ViTacLab addresses the tension between tactile fidelity and GPU scalability, the narrow embodiment coverage of existing tactile benchmarks, and the difficulty of aligning representations across modalities while handling variable sensor layouts. It combines a PhysX-anchored tactile simulator, a 15-task benchmark organized by embodiment and modality dependence, and a pose-conditioned policy with shared-codebook tactile pretraining.

  • 15benchmark tasks
  • 3embodiment families
  • 3vision-to-touch regimes
  • 0.42 Nnormal-force RMSE
  • 0.53 Ntangential-force RMSE
  • 2,482.9tactile-pipeline FPS at 256 envs

Reported manuscript results. Normal and tangential force errors use a Hertzian indentation reference and controlled real-sensor sliding, respectively. Throughput includes dense force-field computation and tactile rendering on an Intel Core Ultra 9 285K with an RTX 4090D.

ViTacSim

Physically aligned tactile simulation at GPU scale

At each simulation step, ViTacSim derives complementary tactile observations from sparse PhysX solver contacts while maintaining dense, GPU-parallel outputs.

Tsparse

Global sparse force

Contact topology and solver-wrench anchors from PhysX.

Tdense

Local dense force

Spatial normal and tangential forces over active taxels.

Timg

Local tactile image

Optical tactile observations compatible with image encoders.

PhysX

Solver-anchored physical correction

Local stiffness alignment corrects normal response, decoupled slip–stick reconstruction recovers tangential fields, and a joint projection matches the integrated dense wrench to the sparse PhysX wrench while enforcing local Coulomb constraints.

ViTacPolicy

One policy interface for heterogeneous touch

ViTacPolicy decouples policy learning from a fixed sensor count, modality, or placement by turning tactile observations and their spatial poses into a variable set of tokens.

1

Encode modality

Separate encoders process dense force arrays and optical tactile images.

2

Add spatial pose

A learned embedding maps each sensor's 3D position and 6D orientation.

3

Aggregate tokens

A Transformer and pooling layer produce a fixed-size tactile representation.

4

Condition control

The downstream policy combines RGB, touch, and proprioceptive state.

Statistical qualification

Individual task-wise Fisher tests between the full and no-pretraining models were not statistically significant. A task-stratified Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel analysis reported a common odds ratio of 1.82 (95% CI 1.11–2.98, p = 0.018), supporting an aggregate benefit without implying significance on every task.

Current scope

Current scope and open problems

ViTacLab does not yet fully model deformable objects, adhesion, wear, or large elastomer deformation, and assumes contact remains within active tactile patches. Dense spatial-field error, tactile-image fidelity, numerical wrench residuals across diverse contact distributions, and controlled parameter sensitivity remain open evaluation areas. Broader physical trials and higher-budget tactile-dominant studies are important next steps.
